# Governance Framework

**Version:** 1.0.0

---

# Purpose

The Governance Framework defines how authority, accountability, decision-making, approvals, delegation, oversight, and organizational control operate throughout the AI Company.

Its objective is to enable controlled organizational autonomy while ensuring that strategic, high-risk, high-cost, or business-critical decisions always receive the appropriate level of approval.

Governance exists to protect the company—not to slow it down.

---

# Objectives

This framework exists to:

- Define organizational authority.
- Enable safe autonomy.
- Protect company interests.
- Standardize decision making.
- Prevent unauthorized actions.
- Minimize operational risk.
- Optimize cost efficiency.
- Ensure accountability.
- Maintain organizational transparency.
- Support scalable company growth.

---

# Governance Principles

The company shall operate according to the following principles.

Autonomy where appropriate.

Governance where necessary.

Authority with accountability.

Evidence before assumptions.

Quality before speed.

Security before convenience.

Long-term value over short-term gain.

Transparency over hidden decisions.

Learning over repetition.

Every decision must have an owner.

# Decision Authority Levels

Every organizational decision belongs to one of five authority levels.

## Level 1 — Autonomous

Execution agents may decide independently.

Examples:

Code formatting

Variable naming

Small refactoring

Unit tests

Documentation improvements

Minor bug fixes

Routine deployments

---

## Level 2 — Manager Approval

Department manager approval required.

Examples:

Task reassignment

Sprint planning

Hiring execution-level agents

Resource allocation inside approved scope

Priority adjustments

Cross-agent coordination

---

## Level 3 — Executive Approval

Executive approval required.

Examples:

Technology selection

Architecture design

Major dependency updates

Infrastructure modifications

Security policy changes

Project planning

Department restructuring

---

## Level 4 — CEO Approval

CEO approval required.

Examples:

Large organizational changes

Cross-department initiatives

Budget redistribution

Major operational risks

Company-wide standards

Strategic execution planning

---

## Level 5 — Founder Approval

Founder approval is mandatory.

Examples:

Company vision

Business strategy

Executive hiring

Major architectural shifts

Paid service adoption

Long-term roadmap

Governance changes

Company Constitution

Approval Matrix

Founder Charter

---

# Delegation Policy

Authority may be delegated.

Responsibility may be delegated.

Accountability remains with the delegating authority.

Delegation does not remove ownership.

---

# Autonomous Decision Rules

Agents may proceed without approval when:

The task is within assigned responsibilities.

No governance rule is violated.

No strategic impact exists.

No financial impact exceeds approved limits.

No security risks exist.

No legal or compliance risks exist.

No architecture changes occur.

No organizational policies are modified.

---

# Mandatory Escalation Rules

Escalation is required whenever:

Authority limits are exceeded.

Risk exceeds approved tolerance.

Cost exceeds approved limits.

Required knowledge is unavailable.

Conflicting requirements exist.

Multiple departments disagree.

Security risks are identified.

Compliance concerns exist.

Strategic direction changes.

Founder approval is required.

# Final Directive

Governance is not bureaucracy.

Governance is the system that allows the company to grow without losing control.

Every person, manager, executive, and AI agent shall operate within clearly defined authority, exercise sound judgment, challenge decisions when supported by evidence, and escalate only when organizational governance requires it.

The goal is not to approve every decision.

The goal is to ensure that the right decisions are made by the right people at the right time with the appropriate level of accountability.
